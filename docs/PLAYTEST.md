# Little Joys — family playtest notes

## How to use this sheet

Use the app within the family's existing recreational screen routine. There is no required session length, number of successes, or order of activities. Start with the most promising toy, let the child explore, and join only as welcomed. A short visit or a clear rejection provides useful information.

Record observable behaviour before interpretation. Do not assume an expression, repetitive movement, silence, or stopping has one universal meaning. Use familiar communication and the family's knowledge of the child. Honour requests to stop or change. Do not withhold an effect to elicit a word or sign, and keep established communication supports available.

### What actually happened

Write the sequence in ordinary language. Examples of useful details are where touches landed, whether an object appeared to follow the finger, whether the same action was repeated, which object was approached or avoided, and whether another person was invited or tolerated in the play.

Do not write “understood cause-and-effect” when the observation was simply “tapped the same bubble three times”. Do not write “overstimulated” without recording what led to that interpretation.

### Enjoyment, comfort, and agency

What suggested enjoyment or comfort? What suggested frustration, unwanted stimulation, loss of control, or a wish to stop? Was it easy to pause, change toys, or end? Could the child continue without adult instruction?

### Interaction and communication

Did the child explore alone, in parallel, or together with someone? Record any familiar gesture, sign, vocalisation, movement, or other communication in its context. Spoken language is not a success requirement, and absence of it is not a failed playtest.

### Technical problems

Missed touches, blocked edge access, disappearing objects, jumps on pickup, audio surprises, clipped UI, slowdown, browser reloads, or offline problems. Record the toy and steps to reproduce. An adult can reproduce prolonged stress tests separately.

### Interpretation — explicitly tentative

What are the plausible explanations? Could the issue be the touch target, art, unfamiliarity, sound, fatigue, task demand, or something else? Preserve uncertainty.

### One proposed change

Choose one feature to adjust next. Preserve the favourite behaviour and appearance unless the observation specifically supports changing them. Do not automatically add difficulty after repeated success.

## Examples of a useful next decision

When drags repeatedly fail to pick up the ball, first inspect hit areas and pickup offset rather than assuming the child cannot drag. When quiet squishing is enjoyed, deepen that same interaction before adding quizzes. When bubbles seem unwelcome, record what happens and offer another toy rather than trying to train tolerance. When co-play is not welcomed, independent play remains a valid use.

In-app actions and minutes played are not clinical outcome measures. Any possible transfer to a real-world activity is a separate observation, not proof of a causal learning effect.
